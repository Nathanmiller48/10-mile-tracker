## 1. Straight answer on the deadline

**Dec 31 is possible but less likely than not. My estimate is somewhat under 50%.** That is a coaching judgment, not a statistic. Reasons:

- You are at ~3 miles continuous on 3–6 miles a week. Getting to 10 by Dec 31 needs the long run to go up about a mile every Saturday with exactly one lighter week and **zero repeated weeks**.
- Hal Higdon's novice 10-mile plan is 10 weeks for first-timers. You have 7.
- Your shins are already talking (1–3/10) at very low mileage, and this plan triples your weekly mileage. In a 1-year study of 874 novice runners (Nielsen et al., JOSPT 2014), those who increased weekly distance by more than 30% had more distance-related injuries, shin splints included, than those who stayed under 10%.
- Your pain is on the **front/outside** of the shin. Sports-medicine protocols treat the anterior tibia as the higher-risk location for a stress injury, and the anterior/lateral compartments are where exertional compartment syndrome usually shows up. That is why the shin rules here are stricter than the usual "up to 5/10 is fine" advice.

**So the plan is built as two tracks with one rule:**

- **Track A** — 10 miles on Thu Dec 31. You stay on it only while every Saturday long run is completed nonstop with shin pain ≤2/10 and no worse the next morning.
- **Track B** — the first time a week has to be repeated, you move to Track B and the 10-miler lands in January (target Sat Jan 23, hard cap Sat Jan 30). Details in section 8.

Switching to Track B is not failing. Forcing Track A through a yellow flag is how a 3-week delay becomes a 3-month one.

**One recommendation outside the plan:** since symptoms are present before the build starts, a single visit to a sports-medicine doctor or running-literate PT in the next two weeks is worth it. It is cheap insurance, and they can check the tibia directly.

## 3. How the plan works

**Running (3 runs a week, never on back-to-back days).**
- Every run is easy: you can speak in full sentences. For a new runner, all running should be easy (the 80/20 model from Seiler and Fitzgerald becomes ~100/0 at your stage). Short strides get added in week 4 only if shins are green.
- Tuesday and Thursday are short. Saturday is the long run and goes up about a mile a week.
- Honest trade-off: Jack Daniels caps the long run at roughly 25–30% of weekly mileage. Yours will be 45–55%. You can't hit his ratio on three runs a week, and I chose three runs over four because bone and the front-of-shin muscles need the day off between impacts more than you need the ratio. Sunday's low-impact aerobic session fills some of the gap. Higdon's novice plans run a similar lopsided ratio.
- The 10-mile attempt comes after a longest training run of 8.5. Higdon's novice half-marathon plan does the same thing (longest run 10, race 13.1).

**Strength (2 short leg exposures + 1 pull day).**
- Blagrove, Howatson and Hayes (Sports Medicine 2018, systematic review of 24 studies) found 2–3 strength sessions a week improved running economy 2–8%. Eihara et al. (2022 meta-analysis) found heavy loads beat plyometrics. So: heavy, low reps, low volume.
- No jumping or plyometrics until shins have been 0/10 for two straight weeks.
- Calf, soleus, tibialis and hip work is in every lower session. Clinical rehab guidelines for shin splints (Sanford, Saint Louis University) build around hip strengthening and single-leg stability, and Warden's bone-stress work is built on graded, symptom-free loading.

**Climbing (3 sessions, but only one hard).**
- Monday hard, Wednesday medium, Friday easy. Friday morning's pull day and Friday evening's easy climb are paired on purpose so Saturday and Sunday are full rest for fingers and elbows before Monday's hard session. Eric Hörst recommends putting weighted pulling on a bouldering or strength day rather than on a rest day.

## 5. The workouts

### RUN-E — Easy run
- 5 min brisk walk, 10 leg swings each way, 10 calf raises, 10 tibialis raises against a wall. Then run.
- Effort: full sentences. If you can't talk, slow down. Pace is whatever that effort gives you.
- Form (these matter for a front-of-shin problem):
  - **Shorter, quicker steps.** Count steps for 30 s on your first run and double it. If you're under ~165, aim 5% higher using a free metronome app. Heiderscheit et al. (2011) showed a 5–10% step-rate increase shortens the stride and cuts braking and joint loading.
  - Land with your foot under you, not out in front with toes yanked up. That toes-up heel strike is what overworks the muscle on the front of the shin.
  - Quiet feet. If you can hear slapping, shorten the step.
  - Avoid long downhills, and don't lace the top of the shoe tight across the ankle.
- 5 min walk after.
- **Strides** (from week 4, only if green for 2 weeks): 4 × 15–20 s smooth accelerations to "fast but relaxed" on flat ground, full walk-back recovery.

### RUN-L — Long run
- Same as the easy run, and start the first mile slower than feels necessary.
- Nonstop is the goal and what counts for milestones. If your form falls apart, a 30–60 s walk break (Galloway's method) is better than limping the distance. The run still counts as training, not as the milestone.
- From 7 miles up: carry water or loop past it, and take 20–30 g of carbs around 45 min and again around 75 min. Practice this on Dec 5 and Dec 19.
- Stop rule: shin hits 3/10 → walk home. See section 7.

### STR-A — Legs + Armor (Monday, 55–60 min)
Heavy, low volume, always 2–3 reps left in the tank. After week 2 you should not be sore past Tuesday morning. If you are, drop a set.

### ARM-B — Armor mini-circuit (Thursday after the run, 12–15 min)
Low-soreness on purpose; it's 48 hours before the long run.

- Tibialis raise: 2 × 15
- Side plank with top-leg lift: 2 × 8/side
- Single-leg balance, eyes closed: 2 × 30 s/side
- Kneeling shin stretch + calf stretch: 2 × 30 s each

### PULL — Lock & Pull (Friday, 45–50 min)
Bar and rings only. No small edges, so fingers are spared.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Warm-up | 8 min | Band pull-aparts 2×15, scapular pull-ups 2×8, band external rotation 2×12, 2 easy sets of 5 pull-ups |
| 1 | Weighted pull-up | 4 × 4 | 1 s pause chin-over-bar, controlled lower, shoulders stay engaged at the bottom. 3 min rest. Start with a load you could do 6–7 times (roughly +10–20% bodyweight). +5 lb when all sets are clean. Stop 2 reps short of failure |

| 3 | Single-arm row, neutral grip (cable or dumbbell) | 3 × 8/side | 1 s pause at the ribs |
| 4 | Hammer curl | 2 × 8 | 3 s lowers. Trains the lock-off muscles (brachialis, brachioradialis); neutral grip is easier on the inner elbow than supinated curls |
| 5 | Antagonist circuit | 2–3 rounds | Push-ups × 12–15, face pulls or band external rotation × 15, light reverse wrist curl × 15 |
| 6 | Tuck front-lever hold (or hanging knee raise × 8) | 3 × 8–10 s | Body tension |

**Why this shape:**
- Hörst's standard is 5×5 weighted pull-ups twice a week, for climbers who can do 10–15 strict pull-ups with no elbow or shoulder history. You qualify. I cut it to 4×4 once a week because you're also climbing three times, and elbows fail from total pulling volume, not from one session.
- Hörst lists uneven-grip pull-ups and one-arm lock-offs as the route to lock-off strength, and antagonist/stabilizer work as standard injury prevention. That's items 2 and 5.
- Lattice's assessment data (733 tests) says pull strength keeps mattering up to about 160% of bodyweight for men, and their own coaches say hard climbing maintains it. So this day is support work, not your main lever at V4. Technique mileage on Wednesday is.

**Upper-body protection rules.** Any inner-elbow, lower-biceps-tendon, shoulder, or finger-pulley ache at 3/10 or more: (1) drop Friday's climb, (2) drop the lock-off ladder, (3) take the weight off the pull-ups. In that order. Sharp finger pain or a pop: stop climbing and get it looked at.

### Climbing sessions
- **CLIMB-H (hard):** 20 min warm-up, then ~45 min of attempts at your limit (V4–V5) with 3–5 min rest between tries. End when quality drops. ≤90 min. Legs were trained that morning, so downclimb instead of dropping from the top.
- **CLIMB-M (medium):** Flash-level volume (V2–V4). Pick styles you avoid. ≤75 min. Leave with gas in the tank.
- **CLIMB-E (easy):** ≤60 min, two grades under max, no projects, no dynos. Skip it whenever life gets busy; it's the first thing to cut.
- No hangboard, no campus board during this plan.

### RESET — Active recovery (Wednesday, 20–30 min, no equipment)
10-min easy walk, then: world's-greatest stretch × 5/side, 90/90 hip switches × 10, couch stretch 60 s/side, knee-to-wall ankle mobility × 10/side, thoracic rotations × 8/side, shoulder circles, forearm extensor and flexor stretch 30 s each.

### AER — Low-impact aerobic (Sunday, 30–45 min)
Bike, elliptical, or incline treadmill walk at conversational effort, then 10 min of the RESET mobility. This adds aerobic work with no pounding on the tibia.

### WALK
15–30 min easy walk. That's the whole session.

## 7. Shin rules

Score pain 0–10 at two moments: the **worst point during the run**, and **the next morning** when you first walk and press along the shin.

| Status | What it looks like | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | 0–2 during. Gone within an hour. Next morning no worse than the morning before | Continue the plan |
| **Yellow** | 3–4 during, **or** still there the next morning, **or** creeping up across three runs in a row | Stop the run when it reaches 3 and walk home. Next scheduled run becomes the same minutes on bike or elliptical. No running for 48–72 h. Keep strength work but drop the weight on calf and tibialis exercises to bodyweight. Restart by **repeating the previous week**, which means **Track B** |
| **Red** | 5 or more, limping, pain when walking, or any item on the doctor list below | No running. Cross-train only if it's pain-free. Book a sports-medicine appointment. Don't run again until daily life has been pain-free for 5 straight days. Restart at less than half your previous weekly mileage |

Two self-checks each Sunday:
1. **Press test.** Run your thumb down the front and inner edge of the shin bone. Soreness spread along the muscle is the familiar kind. One fingertip-sized tender spot on the bone is not.
2. **Hop test.** 10 single-leg hops per side. Any shin pain is a fail.

Where the thresholds come from: Warden, Edwards and Willy (JOSPT 2021) define the right load for a tibial stress injury as one that causes no symptoms during, after, or the next day, and allow a return to running after 5 pain-free days of normal life. The Sanford MTSS guideline restarts running below 50% of prior volume. The looser "up to 5/10 is acceptable" pain-monitoring model (Thomeé, Silbernagel) was built for tendons, not bone, so it is not used here.

### See a doctor if any of these show up

- One spot on the shin bone, small enough to cover with a fingertip, that hurts when pressed
- Pain at rest, at night, or when walking
- Pain on the hop test
- Swelling, warmth, or a lump over the shin
- No improvement after 1–2 weeks of backing off
- **Compartment-syndrome pattern:** tightness, cramping or burning in the front or outside of the shin that starts at about the same point in every run, builds as you continue, and eases within about 15 minutes of stopping. Especially with numbness or tingling on the top of the foot, or the foot slapping the ground
- **Go to the ER** for a tense, swollen lower leg with severe pain that doesn't settle with rest, or numbness that doesn't clear

## 8. Track B — the backup timeline

**Trigger:** any failed gate in section 4.

**Rules (the site computes this; here is the logic):**
1. The Saturday after the trigger, repeat the **longest long run you completed green in the last 3 weeks**, rounded down to a whole mile. After a red, start at half of it.
2. Each Saturday after that adds **1 mile at most**, and never goes past 8.5 before the attempt.
3. Every **4th** week after the switch is a cutback: long run 25% shorter than the week before, rounded to the nearest half mile. The week after a cutback resumes at the pre-cutback distance plus 1.
4. Two weekday runs, each about 40–45% of that week's long run, rounded to the nearest half mile, minimum 2, maximum 4.
5. After a clean **8.5**, the next Saturday is 6, and the Saturday after that is **10**.
6. Strength, pull day, climbing, and recovery days stay exactly as in section 3.
7. Finals and holiday adjustments from section 4 still apply on their dates.
